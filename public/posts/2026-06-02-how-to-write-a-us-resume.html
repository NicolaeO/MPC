How to Write a US-Style Resume

Many talented immigrants send out dozens of applications and hear nothing back — not because they lack experience, but because their resume is written for a different job market. A US resume is a short marketing document, not a complete record of your career. Recruiters often decide in under a minute whether to keep reading, so every line has to earn its place.

Resume vs. CV: What Changes

If you are used to the European or Moldovan CV format (often a Europass template), here is what is different in the US:

Typical European CV US resume
Length 2–4 pages 1 page (2 if you have 10+ years of relevant experience)
Photo Common Never
Personal details Date of birth, nationality, marital status None of these — employers avoid them because of anti-discrimination laws
Focus Duties and responsibilities Measurable achievements
Layout Templates, tables, skill bars Clean single column, simple fonts
References Often listed Not included — provide them only when asked

The Structure That Works

  1. Header — your name, city and state ("Chicago, IL"), phone, email and LinkedIn URL. No full street address.
  2. Summary (optional) — two or three lines on who you are and what you bring: "Financial analyst with 7 years of experience in FP&A and reporting for multi-site retail businesses."
  3. Experience — most recent job first. For each role: title, company, location, dates, and 3–6 bullet points.
  4. Education — degree, school, year. Add the US equivalency if you have an evaluation.
  5. Skills — tools, technologies, certifications and languages.

Write Bullets That Show Results

The single biggest upgrade you can make is turning duties into achievements. Use this formula: action verb + what you did + measurable result.

Instead of… Write…
Responsible for monthly accounting reports Prepared monthly financial reports for 12 retail locations, cutting the close process from 10 days to 6
Worked with customers Resolved 40+ customer requests a day while keeping a 96% satisfaction score
Developed software for the company Built a payment-reconciliation service in Java that eliminated 15 hours of manual work per week
Managed a team Led a team of 6 engineers that shipped the company’s first mobile app, reaching 50,000 users in 6 months
No exact numbers? Estimate honestly. Team size, number of clients, transactions per day, budget managed, time saved, or error rates are all fair game — as long as you could explain the number in an interview.

Start every bullet with a strong past-tense verb: led, built, launched, reduced, increased, automated, negotiated, trained, redesigned, delivered. Avoid "responsible for" and "helped with" — they hide what you actually did.

Translating Your Moldovan Experience

  • Explain unfamiliar companies. A recruiter in Chicago may not know your former employer. Add a short descriptor: "Moldcell — one of Moldova’s largest mobile operators."
  • Translate job titles to US equivalents. Titles do not always map directly. An "economist" at many post-Soviet companies does work closer to a financial analyst or accountant; an "inginer-programator" is a software engineer. Use the title that best describes the work.
  • State your degree’s US equivalent. "Bachelor’s degree in Economics (US equivalent, WES evaluation) — ASEM, Chișinău." See our guide to getting your foreign degree recognized.
  • Convert money to US dollars or use percentages — "grew revenue 35%" means more to a US reader than a figure in lei.
  • List your languages. Romanian and Russian are real assets for many employers: "Languages: Romanian (native), Russian (fluent), English (professional)."
  • Work authorization. You do not have to state your immigration status. If all of your experience is abroad, one line such as "Authorized to work in the US" can remove a recruiter’s doubt.

Get Past the Applicant Tracking System (ATS)

Most medium and large employers run resumes through software before a person reads them. To make sure yours is read correctly:

  • Use a single-column layout with standard headings ("Experience", "Education", "Skills").
  • Avoid tables, text boxes, icons, and putting important details in the page header or footer.
  • Mirror the job posting’s keywords — if it says "financial modeling" and "Power BI", those exact words should appear in your resume (where true).
  • Save as a PDF unless the employer asks for Word, and name the file clearly: Ana-Rusu-Resume.pdf.

Tailor It for Every Application

Keep a longer "master resume" with everything you have done. For each job, copy it and spend 15 minutes reordering bullets so the most relevant achievements come first, and adjust your summary and skills to match the posting. Ten tailored applications usually beat fifty generic ones.

Before You Hit Send

  • One page — or two only if you have 10+ years of relevant experience
  • No photo, age, date of birth, or marital status
  • Every bullet starts with an action verb
  • At least half of your bullets include a number
  • Keywords from the job posting appear in your summary, experience, or skills
  • Consistent dates in US format, e.g. "Mar 2021 – Present"
  • US spelling and spell-check — "organize", not "organise"
  • Saved as a PDF with your name in the file name
  • Your LinkedIn profile matches your resume’s titles and dates

Your Resume’s Only Job

A resume does not need to get you hired — it needs to get you an interview. Keep it short, lead with results, translate your experience into terms a US recruiter understands, and tailor it to each role. Then pair it with networking, which you can read about in How to Find a Job in the USA.
