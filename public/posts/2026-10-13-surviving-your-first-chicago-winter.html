Surviving Your First Chicago Winter

Moldovan winters are cold, but Chicago adds something extra: wind off Lake Michigan, stretches of sub-zero Fahrenheit temperatures, and snow that can last from November into March. With the right clothes, a prepared apartment and car, and a few local rules, winter here is very manageable — and even fun.

Fahrenheit and Wind Chill

US weather reports use Fahrenheit, and in winter they also report the wind chill — how cold it feels on exposed skin when wind is factored in. Wind chill is what matters for frostbite.

Fahrenheit Celsius What it means
32°F 0°C Freezing — wet snow, icy sidewalks
14°F −10°C A normal January day in Chicago
0°F −18°C Seriously cold — cover all skin
−20°F wind chill −29°C Frostbite possible on exposed skin in about 30 minutes
−35°F wind chill −37°C Frostbite possible in about 10 minutes

Dress in Layers

  • Base layer — thermal or merino wool against your skin. Avoid cotton, which stays wet and cold.
  • Middle layer — fleece, wool sweater or a light down jacket for insulation.
  • Outer layer — a windproof, water-resistant parka that covers your hips. The wind is what makes Chicago brutal.
  • Boots — insulated, waterproof, with good grip. Slush and salt destroy regular shoes.
  • Hat, scarf or neck gaiter, and gloves — mittens are warmer than gloves on the coldest days.

You do not need to spend a fortune: Costco, Uniqlo, Burlington and outlet stores carry good winter gear, and end-of-season sales in February and March are the cheapest time to buy for next year. Facebook Marketplace is full of barely-used parkas.

Heat in Your Apartment: Know the Rules

Chicago’s heat ordinance: from September 15 to June 1, landlords who control the heat must keep apartments at least 68°F (20°C) from 8:30 a.m. to 10:30 p.m. and 66°F (19°C) overnight. If your apartment is colder, notify your landlord in writing, and if it is not fixed, call 311 or report it at 311.chicago.gov.
  • If you pay for heat yourself, ask your gas company (Peoples Gas in the city, Nicor Gas in many suburbs) about budget billing, which spreads winter costs evenly across the year.
  • Seal drafts with inexpensive window insulation film and door draft stoppers.
  • Low income? The federal LIHEAP program helps eligible households pay heating bills — apply early in the season, as funds are limited.
  • Space heater safety — keep them away from curtains and bedding, plug them directly into the wall (not a power strip), and never leave one running while you sleep or are away.
  • Never heat your home with a gas oven or stove. Illinois requires carbon monoxide detectors near sleeping areas — test yours before winter.

Driving and Parking in Snow

  • Slow down and leave three times the usual following distance. Brake gently and early — ice is often invisible ("black ice"), especially on bridges.
  • Tires matter most. Good all-season tires are the minimum; winter tires make a dramatic difference if you drive daily.
  • Keep your tank at least half full, and clear snow off the entire car — including the roof — before you drive.
  • Watch for parking bans. Chicago’s Winter Overnight Parking Ban prohibits parking on certain main streets from 3 a.m. to 7 a.m. every night from December 1 to April 1, snow or not. Other streets have a separate ban that starts after 2 inches of snow. Look for the red-and-white signs — towing and storage fees are expensive.
  • "Dibs" — after big storms, you will see chairs and cones "saving" shoveled parking spots. It is a Chicago tradition, though not an official rule. Moving someone’s dibs chair is a good way to make enemies.

Stay Healthy and Sane

Short, gray days can affect anyone’s mood. Get outside in daylight when you can, keep exercising (Chicago Park District fieldhouses and gyms help), and stay social — winter isolation is real, especially far from family. Ask your doctor about vitamin D and whether a light-therapy lamp could help. Get your flu shot in the fall.

Know the warning signs: frostbite — numbness and white or grayish-yellow skin on fingers, toes, ears, nose. Hypothermia — intense shivering, confusion, slurred speech, drowsiness. Get indoors and warm up gradually, and call 911 for hypothermia.

Actually Enjoy It

Chicago does winter well: ice skating at Millennium Park and the Maggie Daley Park skating ribbon, the Christkindlmarket holiday market, ZooLights at Lincoln Park Zoo, sledding hills in city parks, and the frozen lakefront on a sunny day. Winter is also a great time to connect with the Moldovan community for Crăciun and Revelion celebrations.

Your Winter-Ready Checklist

  • A warm, windproof parka that covers your hips
  • Insulated, waterproof boots with good grip
  • Thermal base layers, hat, scarf or gaiter, and gloves or mittens
  • Test your heating and carbon monoxide detector before November
  • Seal drafty windows with insulation film
  • Set up budget billing if you pay for gas heat
  • Pack a winter car kit — ice scraper, snow brush, blanket, phone charger, jumper cables, small shovel
  • Check tires and battery — cold weather kills weak batteries
  • Learn the parking-ban signs on your street
  • Get a flu shot

Prepared Beats Tough

Nobody enjoys a Chicago winter by being tough — they enjoy it by being prepared. Buy the right layers, know your rights on heat, respect the parking bans, drive slowly, and plan things to look forward to. By March, you will be giving advice to the next newcomer.
