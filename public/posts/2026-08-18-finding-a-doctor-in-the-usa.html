Finding a Doctor in the USA

Having health insurance is only half the job — you also need to know where to go when you get sick. In the US you usually cannot just walk into a clinic near your home. Choosing the right place for care can be the difference between a $30 copay and a $3,000 bill.

Start With a Primary Care Physician

A primary care physician (PCP) is your main doctor: annual checkups, vaccines, prescriptions, managing ongoing conditions, and referrals to specialists. PCPs usually practice family medicine (all ages), internal medicine (adults) or pediatrics (children). Nurse practitioners (NPs) and physician assistants (PAs) also provide much of primary care.

Some plans — especially HMOs — require you to choose a PCP and get a referral before seeing a specialist. See Health Insurance in the USA for how plan types work.

How to Find an In-Network Doctor

  1. Use your insurance company’s "Find a Doctor" tool on its website or app. Filter by specialty, location and language — many directories let you search for Romanian- or Russian-speaking doctors.
  2. Call the office to confirm: "Are you accepting new patients?" and "Do you take [your exact plan name]?" Online directories are often out of date.
  3. Check which hospital the doctor is affiliated with — that is usually where you would be referred for tests or specialists.
  4. Book a "new patient" visit early. Waits of several weeks are common, so establish care while you are healthy.

Preventive Care Is Usually Free

Under the Affordable Care Act, most health plans must cover preventive services at no cost when you use an in-network provider — an annual checkup, recommended vaccines, and screenings such as blood pressure, cholesterol and certain cancer screenings.

Watch out: if you bring up a new problem during your free annual visit ("my knee has hurt for a month"), the office may bill a separate diagnostic visit that counts toward your deductible. That is legal — just be aware of it, and ask when you book.

Telehealth, Urgent Care or the ER?

Where Use it for Typical cost with insurance
Telehealth Colds, rashes, prescription refills, simple infections, mental health visits $0–$75
Urgent care Sprains, minor cuts that need stitches, fevers, ear or urinary infections, X-rays $50–$150 copay
Emergency room Chest pain, signs of stroke, trouble breathing, severe bleeding, serious injuries Hundreds to thousands of dollars until you meet your deductible
In a real emergency, call 911 or go straight to the ER. Under federal law (EMTALA), hospital emergency departments must examine and stabilize you regardless of your insurance or ability to pay. Never let cost stop you in a life-threatening situation.

Your First Appointment

  • Bring your photo ID and insurance card, and arrive 15 minutes early to fill out forms.
  • Bring a list of your medications using their active ingredients — brand names in Moldova and Europe often differ from US brand names.
  • Bring vaccination records and any important past medical records, translated if possible.
  • Sign up for the clinic’s patient portal (often called MyChart) to see results, message your doctor and book visits online.

A Free Interpreter Is Your Right

Hospitals and clinics that receive federal funding must provide language assistance at no cost to you. If you are more comfortable in Romanian or Russian, ask for an interpreter when you book and again when you arrive. Medical conversations are too important to rely on a child or friend to translate.

Understanding Your Bills

  • An Explanation of Benefits (EOB) from your insurer is not a bill. Compare it with the bill from the provider — the amounts should match.
  • For a large bill, ask for an itemized bill. Errors and duplicate charges are common.
  • Ask about financial assistance. Nonprofit hospitals are required to have financial assistance (charity care) policies, and many patients qualify for large discounts. Payment plans are almost always available.
  • The No Surprises Act protects you from most surprise out-of-network bills for emergency care and for out-of-network doctors at in-network hospitals. Learn more at cms.gov/nosurprises.

No Insurance? You Still Have Options

  • Community health centers charge on a sliding scale based on income, whether or not you have insurance. Find one at findahealthcenter.hrsa.gov.
  • In Chicago, Cook County Health — the county’s public health system — offers financial assistance programs for uninsured residents.
  • Save on prescriptions by asking for generics, comparing prices with discount programs such as GoodRx, or using Mark Cuban Cost Plus Drugs.

Set It Up Before You Need It

The best time to find a doctor is when you are healthy. Pick an in-network primary care doctor, schedule your free annual checkup, save the address of an in-network urgent care, and install your insurer’s app. When something does happen, you will know exactly where to go — and what it will cost.
